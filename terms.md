## 1. Predictions, not promises

**Seen from Space is provided for information and education. Everything it shows about a future pass is a prediction.**

- Pass times are computed from public orbital data with the standard SGP4 model. They are estimates, and they become less accurate as the orbital data ages.
- <span class="tag will">WILL IMAGE</span> means that, under the mission's standard acquisition plan as the app models it, an image of the point is expected. The app does not read the operators' actual plans and does not know about clouds, manoeuvres, outages or regional exceptions. **It is not a guarantee that an image exists or will exist.**
- <span class="tag can">CAN IMAGE</span> means only that the geometry would allow a commercial satellite to image the point **if a customer ordered it**. The app does not claim that the satellite will image the point, that it did, or that such an image can be bought.
- Sensor parameters (swath, resolution, pointing limits, radar angles) come from public documentation or, where the app marks them "assumed", from a generic model. They may be wrong.

**Do not use the app for navigation, aviation, marine use, surveying, emergencies, security, legal evidence, or any purpose where an error could cause damage, injury or loss.**

## 2. What the app does

It computes when Earth-imaging satellites pass over the points you save, judges whether each pass can produce an image, and shows the publicly available Sentinel-2 images of those points.

It is not a tasking tool, not an archive search, not a live view of the Earth, and not a substitute for the operators' own coverage tools.

## 3. Satellite images

The images shown in the app are **Copernicus Sentinel-2 data**, published by the European Union's Copernicus programme under free, full and open terms, and read from the AWS Open Data programme. The app crops and renders them, so what you see is modified Copernicus Sentinel data.

- You may save and share the images. When you do, keep the attribution the app adds: **"Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data"** with the year.
- The images are provided by their publishers without warranty. They may be cloudy, late, missing, or replaced by a lower-resolution preview; the app says so when it uses a preview.
- At 10 metres per pixel the images do not show people, vehicles or the details of a home. You remain responsible for how you use and share any image.

Imagery from commercial satellites is not public, and the app never shows it.
